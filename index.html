Endless Possibilities

2 October 2026

Back in 2011, the City of Atlanta sold the historic Sears distribution center on Ponce de Leon Avenue, better known as City Hall East, to the real estate investment firm Jamestown. Today, the project is known as Ponce City Market (PCM), an urban development effort so successful, it has become a blueprint for similar initiatives. Yet, unless you are a zoning nerd, as I like to call myself, you will never see this deal. What you likely will see is poncecitymarket.com. There, PCM doesn't try to hurl all the restaurants, spaces, and apartments it has to offer at you. It paints the building's present against the backdrop of its past. Through color temperature, imperative tone, and local credentials, Jamestown persuades tourists, locals, and potential tenants to see PCM as a staple of Atlanta's urban development that can accommodate all aspects of their lives.

I

Color temperature

Warm Cool

Once you open poncecitymarket.com, you're hit with hues of amber coloring the building. Scroll down, and your screen cools down with teal. From the color of sepia and old photographs to the tones of clean new interiors, a tiny motion sells you PCM's entire story. Jamestown is offering both historic grandeur and worldly pleasures, all in one package.

Look closer. An old façade and the title “ENDLESS POSSIBILITIES.” Jamestown leaves us wondering: What does this old building look like on the inside? Our minds are doing the work for the website, and sure enough, anything will feel more authentic in an old shell than in a glass tower. Yet, the feeling of age itself is artificial. Its origin is the amber filter, not the architecture.

The past owns the façade, but what owns it now? Over the building run logos: a stencil, a condensed sans, and a serif. Three completely different identities, all monochrome in black, ordered in a line neatly dipped into the cold of teal. Businesses representing culture and community of Atlanta have been flattened by PCM. The uniqueness of every color is suppressed to conform to one idea. At PCM, the substance comes from the historic feeling. For PCM, businesses are just sleek and practical.

II

Imperative tone

Now look up. Lurking in the corner, wherever you scroll: VISIT. STAY. LIVE. WORK. No one asks you to learn more or explore. Jamestown commands action. One click, no hassle: it's a smart way to capture attention. Read it from left to right, visit → stay → live → work, and you've gone from a tourist to a tenant. You are now a part of PCM's life cycle.

But what has happened to “ENDLESS POSSIBILITIES”? What started as anything you could want ended up as exactly four verbs. For a place with shops and apartments, it's curious that none of them is SHOP or BUY, despite nearly all of them leading to a register or a lease. Jamestown wants you to think that PCM is a place for living, not spending.

III

Local credentials

If all of this hasn't convinced you yet, don't worry. Jamestown has one more weapon in its arsenal: local credentials. “56 LOCALLY OWNED BUSINESSES.” “10 STEPS FROM THE BELTLINE.” That’s ethos for anyone familiar with Atlanta. Not only that, but the numbers add something the rest of the page doesn't: the sound of facts. The number of local businesses or distance from the BeltLine are the first facts you could actually verify, the perfect logos to add credibility.

Challenge accepted. Right next to “56 LOCALLY OWNED BUSINESSES” is a Madewell bag, a national chain owned by the J.Crew Group. Jamestown may preach local ownership, yet its marketing shows clear gaps in that story. “Local,” it turns out, has a lot in common with amber hues. Both are less a fact than a feeling.

What is poncecitymarket.com selling then? Is it restaurants or apartments? Neither. It's selling a feeling. Jamestown wants you to see a historic landmark, “local” brands, and a place that can envelop your entire life.

But remember! We began in a public building, sold with a promise to “restore the building's former character.” Is this it? An amber filter over an old façade, a row of flattened logos, and a chain-store bag next to “locally owned”? The building may have been restored, but its online presence has replaced its former character with a thought-out brand.

Now think of the legacy. PCM became a blueprint, and blueprints get copied. So, next time you visit PCM or poncecitymarket.com, ask yourself: Is this how we want Atlanta's history to be remembered? Hopefully not. Is everyone who offers endless possibilities just trying to sell you something? Probably yes. Should you automatically dismiss them? No. Just consider what promises will be kept and whose past will be tinted.

A note on the design

The subject persuades with colour. The presentation must not, or the two become indistinguishable. Five choices follow from that.

Colour temperature is encoded as position

  • The claim: the page moves warm to cool as you descend it.
  • Rail colour at any height = the colour under discussion there.
  • Filled length = reading position.
  • Checkable against the captures without leaving the sentence.

Named evidence is tied to where it sits

  • Four phrases name something visible in a capture.
  • Each is underlined and joined to its box by a hairline.
  • Hovering brightens both ends.
  • Nothing is written onto the captures — they stay as taken.

The navigation is reproduced as the site sets it

  • The site's own weight, spacing and alignment, on the hero's ground.
  • No wordmark, no added labels.
  • Underlines accumulate left to right as you scroll; hover jumps.
  • The only element reproduced rather than photographed.

The background sits outside the range being analysed

  • Capture 1: 0.31 mean saturation.
  • Capture 2: 0.21.
  • Page background: 0.02.
  • Any colour the reader registers comes from the evidence.

Sources stay inside the sentence that uses them

  • Four assertions rest on documents, not on the website.
  • A marker opens the source where it is cited and links out.
  • Numbers index the works cited, which is alphabetical.
  • Same source, same number, wherever it appears.

One test throughout: a reader can check the argument against the evidence.
